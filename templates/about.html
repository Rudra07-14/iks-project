Collegiate Project Documentation

Ayurvedic Classification as a Rule-Based Expert System

An academic interdisciplinary demonstration synthesizing Indian Knowledge Systems (IKS) with foundational Artificial Intelligence and production rule inference.

Part 1: The Domain

Indian Knowledge Systems (IKS)

Ayurveda, formalized in classical treatises such as the Charaka Samhita and Sushruta Samhita, provides an empirical framework for categorizing human constitution (Prakriti). Individual variance is understood through combinations of three energetic principles: Vata, Pitta, and Kapha.

Part 2: The Computation

Rule-Based Expert Systems

In classic AI, expert systems represent domain knowledge as structured IF <condition> THEN <action> rules. They process asserted user facts through an inference engine to deduce conclusions with full explainability and mathematical determinism.

Computational Complexity & Inference Algorithm

Time Complexity
O(n)

Where n = 8 (number of questions). Each fact is looked up in the hash-based rule dictionary in constant O(1) time.

Space Complexity
O(n)

Linear space to store working memory scores (3 counters) and n explanation audit records in memory.

Deterministic Tie Handling

In the event that two or three Doshas obtain identical aggregate scores (for example, 4 Vata and 4 Pitta), the academic algorithm applies a deterministic tie-breaker based on dictionary precedence order:

Precedence Resolution: Vata (वात) > Pitta (पित्त) > Kapha (कफ)

Assumptions and Scope Limitations

  • Predefined Static Knowledge Base: All rules are manually codified from educational Ayurvedic references and remain static during execution.
  • 8 Fixed Categories: The system models 8 specific somatic and behavioral parameters, rather than a clinical multi-hundred parameter diagnostic assessment.
  • Equal Unit Weighting: Each fired rule contributes an equal score increment of +1 to its associated Dosha.
  • No Dual-Prakriti Representation: While traditional Ayurveda recognizes bi-doshic (Dwandwaja) Prakriti (e.g. Vata-Pitta), this collegiate project models primary single-dosha dominance for algorithmic clarity.
  • Absence of Black-Box ML: The system intentionally avoids machine learning, neural networks, or generative AI to preserve deterministic explainability.
