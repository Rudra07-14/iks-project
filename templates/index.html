Ayurvedic Foundations

The Three Biological Energies (Tridosha)

In classical Ayurveda, Prakriti refers to an individual’s unique physiological and psychological constitution, determined by the relative predominance of three primary Doshas.

वात · Vata Air + Ether

The Principle of Movement

Lightness, quickness, variability, and movement. Governs all biological circulation, breathing, motor impulses, and sensory perception.

Primary Qualities: Light, dry, cold, mobile, subtle
In Expert System: Associated with slender frame, quick thoughts, irregular appetite
पित्त · Pitta Fire + Water

The Principle of Transformation

Heat, intensity, focus, and transformation. Governs enzymatic digestion, thermoregulation, cellular metabolism, and sharp analytical intellect.

Primary Qualities: Hot, sharp, light, oily, spreading
In Expert System: Associated with moderate build, strong appetite, sharp intellect
कफ · Kapha Earth + Water

The Principle of Stability

Stability, heaviness, calmness, and steadiness. Provides musculoskeletal structure, cellular cohesion, lubrication, immunity, and endurance.

Primary Qualities: Heavy, slow, steady, solid, cold, soft
In Expert System: Associated with broad frame, calm temper, deep enduring memory

Why Build an Ayurvedic Expert System?

Traditional Indian Knowledge Systems contain centuries of systematic observational taxonomies. In Ayurveda, individual constitutional classification (Prakriti) is codified through distinct somatic, metabolic, and behavioral markers.

In classic Computer Science, a Rule-Based Expert System is one of the foundational paradigms of Artificial Intelligence. It models problem-solving using an explicit knowledge base of IF-THEN rules rather than statistical probabilities or opaque neural weights.

By formalizing Ayurvedic principles into executable production rules, this collegiate project demonstrates that traditional knowledge can be systematically formalized, tested, and made completely explainable through computation.
